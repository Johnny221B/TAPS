01 / THE INSIGHT

Not just more noise.
Better timing.

A new sentence is not always a new idea.

Token-level randomness can change wording while leaving the underlying semantic direction largely unchanged. Diffusion language models expose another control axis: the denoising process itself.

The paper’s probing experiments reveal a temporal division of labor. Early interventions have a stronger influence on high-level semantics; later interventions more often alter lexical realization. TAPS places exploration where the generation is still plastic, then withdraws it before refinement.

THE BIGGER PICTURE / WHY DIVERSITY MATTERS

More samples.
More possibilities?

Scaling the number of answers is useful only when it expands what the model explores.

For a large language model, diversity is more than creative wording. In mathematics, it can mean a different sequence of operations leading to a correct result. In code, it can mean a different valid program structure. Redundant samples repeatedly spend compute on the same region of the solution space.

This becomes especially relevant in recursive self-improvement (RSI) and self-training: a system that generates its own candidates needs alternatives worth evaluating, selecting and learning from. If candidate generation keeps returning the same strategies, the search has fewer opportunities to discover useful alternatives. Open-ended self-improvement systems such as the Darwin Gödel Machine likewise explore multiple branches rather than relying on a single lineage.

IMPLICATION / GROUP-RELATIVE LEARNING

A group needs informative reward differences.

GRPO learns from rewards relative to the rest of a group. When every rollout receives the same reward, centered rewards—and the reward-based policy-gradient contribution—vanish in the usual stabilized formulation. This is the advantage-collapse problem.

TAPS motivates a possible intervention at rollout collection: broaden the candidate paths before evaluation. If that exploration produces useful reward differences, it may help avoid uninformative groups. The paper establishes diversity among correct solutions; reducing zero-advantage groups or improving RL training remains to be tested.

03 / THE METHOD

Explore nearby.
Stay grounded.

Time-Annealed Perturbation Sampling
A lightweight intervention in conditioning space.

01

Perturb the condition

Sample a nearby prompt-derived embedding during the active exploration window.

Ẽ(t) = E + σ(t)ε
02

Constrain the excursion

Rescale to match distribution statistics, mix with the original condition and preserve per-token norms.

rescale → mix → project
03

Anneal before refinement

Reduce perturbation strength and return to the original embedding outside the active window.

σ(t) → 0
Why perturb conditioning rather than only tokens? +

TAPS samples from neighboring conditional distributions, giving different generations distinct opportunities to form high-level semantic plans. Annealing limits interference with the subsequent refinement stage. Token-level masking is also evaluated in the paper, but embedding-level perturbation offers a more favorable quality–diversity balance in that comparison.

Scope, cost & limitations +

TAPS is an inference-time method for diffusion language models, not a new training objective. Perturbation strength and timing remain configuration choices. Stronger exploration can reduce quality, and the best settings may vary by backbone and task. Reported gains are empirical, not universal guarantees. Effects on downstream RL training remain to be evaluated.

The paired cost experiment reports 19.980 ± 0.010 seconds/sample for the base model and 20.012 ± 0.006 seconds/sample for TAPS, with the same 16,830 MB peak memory. Setup: 30 trials, one fixed GSM8K prompt, 256 generated tokens, 128 denoising steps (Table 9).
